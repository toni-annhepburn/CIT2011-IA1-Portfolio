Introduction

For this section of my portfolio, I read two chapters from The Richest Man in Babylon by George S. Clason: Seven Cures for a Lean Purse and The Five Laws of Gold.

Reading these two chapters made me think about money differently. Before reading them, I mostly thought that managing money was mainly about how much a person earns. The book made me realize that what you do with the money you already have is just as important. Even though the language in the book is old-fashioned, the lessons can still be applied today. As a university student, I may not be earning a full salary yet, but I can still start developing better financial habits now instead of waiting until I am older.

Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

What I Learned

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse taught me that building financial stability is something that happens step by step. One of the main lessons is to save part of what you receive instead of spending everything. The chapter also talks about controlling expenses, making your money grow, protecting your savings, planning for a home and future income, and improving your ability to earn.

The lesson that stood out to me the most was controlling expenses because I like shopping online and sometimes it is easy to see something I like and want to buy it even though I do not actually need it. It made me realize that there is a difference between something I want and something that is really necessary. I do not think the lesson means that I should never enjoy my money, but I should have a limit and make sure I am not spending everything without thinking ahead.

How I Can Apply It to My Life

I can apply these lessons by becoming more intentional with the money I receive. For example, I could set aside a small amount before I start shopping or spending on other things. I could also make a simple budget so I know what I can afford instead of just spending and then checking what is left afterwards.

Another cure that relates strongly to me is increasing my ability to earn. I am currently studying Computer Network and Security, so completing my degree, improving my networking and cybersecurity skills, and gaining more experience can increase the opportunities available to me later. My goal is to work in the technology field, possibly for a company such as Google or Microsoft. This chapter made me see my education as an investment in myself and not just something I am doing to get a degree.

The Five Laws of Gold

What I Learned

The Five Laws of Gold made me understand that having money and knowing how to manage money are two different things. A person can receive a lot of money and still lose it if they do not know what they are doing. The laws teach that a person should save regularly, allow their money to grow, seek advice from people who actually understand money, and avoid putting money into things they do not understand.

I especially liked the warning about opportunities that promise unrealistic profits. Today there are so many advertisements, social media posts and online offers telling people that they can make money quickly. Sometimes something can look convincing even when it is not a good decision. This chapter reminded me that making money slowly and safely can be better than taking a big risk just because something sounds exciting.

How I Can Apply It to My Life

I can apply the Five Laws of Gold by doing more research before making financial decisions and not trusting something just because it promises fast results. Since I am studying in the area of cybersecurity, I already understand why it is important to be careful about what you trust online.

I would want to understand an investment properly before putting my money into it, and if I did not understand it, I would rather ask someone who has real knowledge and experience. When I eventually start earning a regular income, I also want to make saving something automatic instead of only saving whatever happens to be left at the end of the month. I think that would help me become more disciplined and give me more security for the future.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from both chapters is that being prepared makes a big difference. That stood out to me because I am already the type of person who likes to know what I am doing before I start something. With school assignments, I like having the instructions organized and making sure everything is completed properly before I submit it.

I now see that money can be treated in a similar way. Instead of waiting until there is a problem, I can have a plan for saving, spending and improving myself. I also learned that becoming financially stable is not something that happens all at once. It comes from small decisions that are repeated consistently.

If I start developing those habits while I am still a student, I think it will make managing my finances easier when I begin my career and have more responsibilities. I still want to enjoy things such as shopping, trying new food and doing the things I like, but the main lesson for me is that enjoying money and planning for the future do not have to be opposites. I just need to be more disciplined about how I balance both.
