My Personality

Organized and Detail-Oriented

One of my strongest personality traits is being organized. I like things to be neat, tidy and structured. This is very important to me when completing school assignments because I like to follow the instructions exactly as they are given. Before I submit my work, I normally check it to make sure everything is in the correct place and that I did not leave anything out.

This also affects how I work in groups. I prefer when everyone follows the instructions and completes their part properly. When something is not done correctly, it bothers me because I know it can affect the final grade. I usually end up checking or fixing the work because I feel better submitting something that is properly organized.

Prepared and Focused

I like to be prepared before going somewhere or doing something important. For example, interviews make me nervous, so being prepared helps me feel more confident. I use the same approach with school. I prefer knowing what is expected before I start an assignment instead of rushing at the last minute without understanding the instructions.

I also prefer studying late at night because there are fewer distractions. During the day it can be difficult for me to concentrate, but late at night I can focus better and work through my assignments in a quieter environment.

Simple, Calm and Balanced

I like simple things that still look good. I like the colour pink, classy styles and simple outfits that look cool without doing too much. I think this also describes the way I prefer to work because I like things that are clean, clear and easy to understand.

Outside of school, I enjoy shopping online, trying new food and watching football. Arsenal is my favourite football team. I also enjoy driving when I need to clear my mind. These hobbies give me a break from school and help me relax before returning to my assignments with a clearer mind.

A Person I Look Up To

T. Harv Eker

T. Harv Eker is an author, businessman and motivational speaker who is known for his work on personal development, success and financial mindset. He is the author of Secrets of the Millionaire Mind.

Eker grew up in Toronto and started working from a young age. Before becoming successful, he tried several different businesses. He later built a successful fitness-store business, but after becoming wealthy he lost much of his money through poor financial decisions. Instead of giving up, he studied the habits and mindset connected with success and used those experiences in his teaching.

My Favourite Quote

“If you’re only willing to do what’s easy, life will be hard; but if you’re willing to do what’s hard, life will be easy.”

- T. Harv Eker

Why I Look Up To Him

I like this quote because it reminds me that avoiding difficult things does not make them disappear. Sometimes doing something properly takes more time and effort, but it can prevent bigger problems later.

This relates to the way I approach school. I like to be prepared, follow instructions and make sure my assignments are properly completed before I submit them. Even when something is difficult, I would rather spend the time trying to understand it and fix it than submit something that I know is not right.

Eker's story also shows me that making mistakes does not mean a person has to stop trying. He experienced both success and failure and continued learning from his experiences. That is one reason I look up to him. His quote encourages me to deal with difficult things now so that I can make life easier for myself in the future.

LLMs: A Tool, Not a Crutch

The statement that large language models such as ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot should be used as a tool and not a crutch means that students should use AI to support their learning, not to replace their own thinking. I think there is a big difference between asking for help understanding something and allowing a tool to do everything for you.

As a student, I think AI is most useful when I am confused about a topic and need it explained in a simpler way. For example, in Web Programming I can use an AI tool to explain the difference between margin and padding, explain why a piece of HTML is not working, or help me understand an error message. I can then go back to my notes, course material or websites recommended by my lecturer and check the information for myself.

AI can also help with planning. If an assignment has many instructions, it can help break the instructions into smaller steps so that I can work through them one at a time. This is useful to me because I prefer my schoolwork to be organized and clearly structured. However, I still need to understand what I am doing. Copying an answer or code without understanding it would not help me if I was later asked to explain my work.

There are also risks to depending too much on AI. An AI system can provide information that is incorrect, incomplete or not suitable for a particular assignment. For that reason, students should verify important information and follow the rules given by their lecturer. AI should not be used to avoid learning the basic skills that the assignment is supposed to teach.

Overall, I believe LLMs can improve my learning when I use them responsibly. I can use them as a tutor when I need another explanation, as a guide when I am stuck, or as a way to check my understanding. The final responsibility is still mine. I need to think, practise, check the information and make sure I can explain the work I submit. To me, that is what it means to use AI as a tool rather than a crutch.
